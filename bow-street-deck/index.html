How to go ahead
Timing

We can start right away, and timing matters

The footings have to be poured before the cold sets in. Concrete cures through a chemical reaction that slows sharply in cold weather, and if it freezes in the first few days it can lose much of its strength for good.

The cut-off: overnight lows staying above 5 °C

That's the point where building standards treat concrete work as cold-weather work, needing heaters and insulated blankets. In the Halifax area, nights usually start dropping below 5 °C in late October.

Once the footings are in, framing, decking, railings and stairs can carry on into colder weather. If the footings miss this window, the deck would most likely wait until spring. To keep this year's timeline, please confirm by [date] so the permit can go in right away.

01 · Design

Built from your drawings

Your house plans very likely already show this deck. If you can share them, the CAD work is quick: the deck is modelled from your drawings rather than designed from scratch.

The model's main job is the bill of materials: every board, hanger, bolt and screw counted from the model, so the price and the lumber order come from the same place. It also confirms post and footing locations, checks spans against Nova Scotia code tables, and lays out the stairs.

The model shown here is built from photos of the house, so sizes are approximate until they're measured on site or checked against your drawings.

02 · Scope

What's included

  • Deck modelled in CAD from your existing drawings, with a full bill of materials
  • Permit drawings and HRM building permit
  • Seven 10" round concrete piers to 4 ft below grade (below frost)
  • 6×6 posts no more than 8 ft apart, with triple 2×10 beams along the back and the right side
  • 4×4 knee braces at 45° from each post up to the beam, so the posts can't roll over along the beam
  • 2×6 diagonal V-bracing under the joists, tying the frame back to the house so it can't rack
  • 2×8 joists at 16" on centre, off the ledgers already on the back and side walls
  • Solid 2×8 blocking between the joists over both beams and at mid-span on the back section, as the building code requires
  • 5/4×6 pressure-treated decking, about 290 sq ft, with 3/8" gaps for drainage
  • 42" guard railing on all open sides, about 54 ft, with 2×2 wood balusters
  • 6 ft wide stair off the end of the side section: 2×12 stringers hung from a doubled 2×12 stair header and tied together underneath, closed risers, and a guard with a 2×6 handrail on the open side, down to a 6 ft concrete landing cut level into the slope
  • Patio stones under the back deck at the walkout door, about 250 sq ft on a compacted gravel base, out past the footings
  • Hidden deck fasteners (no screw heads on the deck surface); all fasteners rated for treated lumber, stainless with cedar
  • Deliveries, equipment rental, and site cleanup

04 · Schedule

How the job runs

About 115–170 hours of work in total, spread over roughly four to six weeks, including permit time and concrete curing.

  1. Measure and model. Check your drawings against the site, model the deck, produce the bill of materials, your sign-off.
  2. Permit. Drawings submitted to HRM; work starts once approved.
  3. Footings. Dig seven holes to 4 ft, set forms, pour while nights are still above 5 °C. Concrete cures for several days.
  4. Framing. Posts, beams, joists, hangers and V-bracing.
  5. Decking. Boards installed and trimmed.
  6. Railings and stairs. Guards, stair stringers, treads and handrails; landing cut in and poured.
  7. Patio. Excavate under the back deck, compact the gravel base, lay the stones.
  8. Final inspection and cleanup. HRM inspection, site cleared.
05 · Terms

Assumptions and exclusions

This estimate assumes

  • Sizes, heights and slopes estimated from photos hold up when measured on site
  • The existing ledgers on the back and side walls are sound, properly flashed, and fastened for a deck of this size
  • The deck surface is about 8 ft above the walkout grade
  • Footings can be dug with a power auger to frost depth
  • Clear access for deliveries to the back yard
  • The slope at the side can be cut level for the landing with a low retaining edge

Not included

  • Rock or ledge removal beyond normal digging
  • Ledger repair or replacement, if needed
  • Stain or sealer (wood should dry for a season first)
  • Composite decking, glass railings, lighting
  • Landscaping around the finished deck
06 · Next steps

Going ahead

This is a preliminary estimate based on photos. Going ahead depends on agreeing the scope of work and the project schedule; here's how it firms up:

  1. Site measure. A visit to measure the deck height, wall lengths, the existing ledgers and the slope of the yard, and to check your house plans if you have them.
  2. Scope and schedule. We agree exactly what's included (deck, stair, patio, options like cedar or aluminum balusters) and set the schedule, starting with the footing pour before overnight lows drop below 5 °C.
  3. Firm price and contract. The model and bill of materials are updated from the measurements, and we draw up a written contract with the agreed scope, schedule and final price.
  4. Payment. You pay for materials directly, at cost with the receipts, as they're ordered. One third of the labour (about $0) is due before work starts, with the balance on completion.

Any changes to the design are priced before the work is done.
