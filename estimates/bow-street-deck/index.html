How to go ahead
Timing

We can start right away, and timing matters

The footings have to be poured before the cold sets in. Concrete cures through a chemical reaction that slows sharply in cold weather, and if it freezes in the first few days it can lose much of its strength for good.

The cut-off: overnight lows staying above 5 °C

That's the point where building standards treat concrete work as cold-weather work, needing heaters and insulated blankets. In the Halifax area, nights usually start dropping below 5 °C in late October.

Once the footings are in, framing, decking, railings and stairs can carry on into colder weather. If the footings miss this window, the deck would most likely wait until spring. To keep this year's timeline, please confirm by [date] so the permit can go in right away.

04 · Schedule

How the job runs

About 80–110 hours of work in total, spread over roughly three to four weeks, including permit time and concrete curing.

  1. Measure and design. Site visit, 3D model, your sign-off on the layout.
  2. Permit. Drawings submitted to HRM; work starts once approved.
  3. Footings. Dig, set forms, pour while nights are still above 5 °C. Concrete cures for several days.
  4. Framing. Posts, beam, joists, hangers and bracing.
  5. Decking. Boards installed and trimmed.
  6. Railings and stairs. Guards, stair stringers, treads and handrails.
  7. Final inspection and cleanup. HRM inspection, site cleared.
05 · Terms

Assumptions and exclusions

This estimate assumes

  • The existing ledger is sound, properly flashed, and fastened for a deck of this size
  • The deck surface is about 9 ft above grade
  • Footings can be dug with a power auger to frost depth
  • Clear access for deliveries to the back yard

Not included

  • Rock or ledge removal beyond normal digging
  • Ledger repair or replacement, if needed
  • Stain or sealer (wood should dry for a season first)
  • Composite decking, aluminum or glass railings, lighting
  • Landscaping around the finished deck
